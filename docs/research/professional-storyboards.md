# Professional storyboards and short-form videos, compared with Creatro

Research report, 20 Nov 2026. Nothing in the app or the playbook has been changed. Kartik decides what to apply.

The short version: Creatro's storyboard already has the right ingredients: real footage traced in pencil,
red movement marks, typed notes, an animatic and a PDF. What makes it look amateur is **density**. Every panel
says everything at once, in the frame itself: a big zoom box, four corner arrows, burst marks, cursor ticks,
hand-written sentences across the drawing, and labels sitting on the subject. Professional boards show one idea
per panel. They draw only movement inside the frame and put the words underneath. The videos have a similar
problem. The playbook asks for variety by quota (4 layouts, a SFX every 2-3 s, effects on titles). Top
explainer creators do the opposite: one consistent visual world, one accent colour, small clean captions, and
a visual explanation for every claim.

Six Cleo Abram reels (sections 2.1 and 2.1b) and one editorial finance reel (2.1c) were studied frame by frame. The biggest new lesson is how she
presents research: documents as full-frame evidence, in a fixed order (coverage, source, key number, the paper,
the check, the critic), with the accent highlighter on exactly the words being said.

The prioritised list of changes is in section 5 (items 1-33).

## 1. Professional storyboards: what they do

### 1.1 Two kinds of board
- **Agency or pitch board.** A few key frames that sell the idea, often in colour, with the copy typed under
  each frame ([Studiobinder: commercial storyboard template](https://studiobinder.com/templates/storyboards/commercial-storyboard-template),
  [Wikipedia: storyboard artist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Storyboard_artist)).
- **Shooting board.** Every shot in order, quick and loose, usually black and white. It shows the shooting style,
  camera moves, edit and effects. Creatro's board is a shooting board that also has to sell the idea to one
  person (the user). It should look like a shooting board: monochrome pencil with sparing colour, and typed
  production notes.

### 1.2 Panel content and layout
- Each panel has a **number**, the **picture** and, *under* the picture, the **notes**: what happens (action), what
  is heard (dialogue/VO, SFX, music), and the camera
  ([StudioBinder: storyboard notes](https://www.studiobinder.com/blog/storyboard-notes-annotations/),
  [StudioBinder: how to make a storyboard](https://www.studiobinder.com/blog/how-to-make-a-storyboard/)).
  Notes carry what the picture can't show: motivation, sound and some camera moves.
- Numbering is scene.shot ("5.6") or a shot number plus panel letters (12A, 12B) when one shot needs several
  drawings, for example the start and end of a move. Without numbers nobody can talk about a shot
  ([NFI](https://www.nfi.edu/?p=1408), [Videomaker](https://www.videomaker.com/article/f2/15415-how-to-make-a-storyboard-storyboard-lingo-techniques/)).
- Timing is on every panel: its duration, and the running time across the board
  ([Storyflow: commercial storyboard](https://storyflow.so/blog/commercial-storyboard-template-frames-client-approval-2026)).
- On-screen text ("SUPER" in agency language) is drawn where it will really sit, without fighting the composition.
- Sheets hold 3-4 panels for detail work or 6-12 to judge rhythm. A 30-second spot often goes on one thumbnail
  page so the pacing can be seen at once ([Prolific Studio](https://prolificstudio.co/blog/storyboard-template/),
  [Boords templates](https://boords.com/storyboard-template)). Frames match the delivery aspect.
- Ad scripts and boards share the old **two-column AV format**: picture on the left, sound on the right
  (VO: "…", SFX: …, MUSIC: …). It is still how agencies read a board.

### 1.3 How movement is marked
- **Arrows** are the basic symbol, for the camera, the subject or an object
  ([StudioBinder: storyboard arrows](https://www.studiobinder.com/blog/storyboard-arrows-meaning/)).
- **Camera arrows sit at the frame edge. Subject or object arrows sit inside, next to the thing that moves**
  ([Storyboard That: camera shots](https://www.storyboardthat.com/articles/f/camera-shots)). Camera lines are
  drawn thicker. Some boards give object motion a different colour.
- **Pan**: arrows along the top and bottom edges in the pan's direction, or the start and end frames drawn with an
  arrow between them. **Tilt**: a curved arrow on the side. **Combined moves** use one bent arrow (an "L" for tilt
  down then pan right). A **head turn** is a small curved arrow.
- **Zoom**: four arrows at the corners, pointing in (zoom in) or out (zoom out). Arrows over one area mean a zoom
  onto that area.
- **Frame within frame**: for a push-in or pull-out, draw the start frame and the end frame, with arrows from
  one to the other ([Videomaker](https://www.videomaker.com/article/f2/15415-how-to-make-a-storyboard-storyboard-lingo-techniques/),
  [Toon Boom Storyboard Pro: camera moves](https://docs.toonboom.com/help/storyboard-pro-22/storyboard/camera/about-camera-move.html)).
  Zoom (a lens change) and dolly/push-in (the camera moves) are different moves with different words.
- Arrows are always backed by a few words of description, and those words are kept minimal.

### 1.4 Drawing style
- **One idea per drawing.** The audience can only look at one thing at a time. A drawing with several ideas
  loses some of them, so a new idea gets a new panel
  ([Creative Bloq: storyboard tips](https://creativebloq.com/advice/storyboard-tips)).
- **Clear, not clean.** Boards must read quickly. Thumbnails are rough, and detail comes later.
- **Silhouette test.** Fill the subject with solid tone: if the action still reads, the panel works. Negative
  space around the subject is what makes it readable ([Doug Lefler: silhouettes](https://douglefler.com/drawing-tips-silhouettes/)).
- **Ridley Scott's "Ridleygrams"** (the most-cited director boards) are quick ink and pencil contours with strong
  light and dark, dynamic angles and mood. They have almost no text in the frame
  ([No Film School: Napoleon storyboards](https://nofilmschool.com/napoleon-storyboards),
  [Boords: Ridley Scott storyboards](https://boords.com/blog/10-best-ridley-scott-storyboards-to-give-you-inspiration)).
- The 180° rule: keep the camera on one side of the action line so cuts don't flip direction.

### 1.5 Typography on boards
Production software (StudioBinder, Boords, ShotPro, Toon Boom) types its notes in a plain sans under the frame.
Hand lettering, when used, means short capitals for movement labels ("PUSH IN", "PAN →"), never
sentences across the picture. Standard shorthand: ECU, CU, MCU, MS, WS, EWS, OTS, POV, INSERT; PUSH IN, PULL
OUT, PAN, TILT, TRACK, WHIP; CUT TO, DISSOLVE, SMASH CUT; VO, SFX, MUS, SUPER.

## 2. Top short-form and explainer creators: what they do

### 2.1 Kartik's reference: Cleo Abram reel (Instagram, Oct 2026, 56 s, 1080×1920)
The reel is [instagram.com/reel/De4n1K5owtx](https://www.instagram.com/reel/De4n1K5owtx/) ("We're missing the best
place to find aliens"). I sampled about 25 frames across it.

| What | What it does |
|---|---|
| Hook (0-3 s) | Her face, close, very expressive, saying the claim straight away: "We might be missing the best place to find aliens!" No title card, logo or intro. The first visual cuts in at about 3 s. |
| Structure | Claim, then the rule ("habitable zone = where liquid water can exist", shown as a diagram), then the turn back to camera ("But we're super biased"), then the proof (a real lab photo of ionic liquids), then the new idea, then the payoff ("…than we think!"), then the next step (STARSHADE). |
| Face vs. visuals | About 25-30 % of sampled frames show her face, about 70 % show full-frame visuals. She comes on camera **at the turns** (the claim, "but", "unlike water"). The visuals carry the explanation. |
| Layouts | Only two: her full frame, and a full-frame visual. No split screens, floating cards or cut-outs. |
| Visual world | One consistent 3D world: glass lab flasks on a dark reflective grid floor, DNA, glowing planets, star fields. Every abstract idea gets a **concrete object** (water as a flask labelled H₂O, the habitable zone as coloured rings around a star). |
| Labels | Labels are **stuck to the object** as small tags in her brand lime (`H₂O`, `IONIC LIQUID`, `IONIC LIQUIDS` over the lab photo), black text on a lime box, slightly tilted. Diagram text follows the shape ("TOO HOT" and "HABITABLE ZONE" curved along the orbit ring). |
| Titles | Rare and big: one hero word, "STARSHADE", in heavy lime sans with **no effect** (no shadow, outline or glow). |
| Captions | One line of 3-5 words, **white, bold, all caps, small** (about 4 % of frame width per letter height, roughly 40-45 px on 1080). **No pill or box**, just a soft shadow. Always in the same place, about 78 % down the frame, inside the safe zone. They never change style. |
| Colour | Neutral and dark frames, plus **one accent colour** (lime, about #D4FF3A) for every label, title and the follow button. The accent is her brand colour, the same as her avatar. |
| Credits | Tiny "* SOURCE: NASA" or a source line top left whenever a real image or paper appears. It builds trust. |
| Transitions | Hard cuts. No whips, glitches or flashes in the frames sampled. |
| CTA | A faded lime "FOLLOW" pill at the midpoint (about 26 s), not at the end. |

Cleo explains how she works: she starts from the **visual that makes the idea understandable**, before the
script, a habit from Vox. The team is four people: Cleo, an animator/producer who makes the 3D, a science
producer and an editor ([Video Consortium interview](https://videoconsortium.org/mag/episode-8-cleo-abram),
[Colin and Samir](https://rosetta.to/u/colinandsamir/how-cleo-abram-dominated-youtube-in-under-1-year),
[YouTube blog](https://blog.youtube/creator-and-artist-stories/5-huge-if-true-moments-for-cleo-abram/)).

**How she presents a research paper (the 722 papers reel)**, in order:
1. **Face, pointing at camera**, saying the claim (0-2 s).
2. **The news coverage** as a full-screen page: the date line, then the headline **zoomed in until it bleeds off
   the right edge**, with a **lime highlighter over exactly the words being said**. There is no frame, no card and
   no browser chrome, just the page as the whole picture.
3. **The papers' figures** as a stack of white page cut-outs that land one on another, slightly tilted, with soft
   shadows, over a blurred 3D background. The volume of evidence is felt rather than read.
4. **The primary source** (the lab's own announcement page): the title highlighted in lime.
5. **The key number in the body text**, pushed in close so a few lines fill the frame, and only that phrase
   highlighted ("4,000 problems").
6. **The papers themselves**: dense pages, two stacked, held long enough to register "real", not long enough to
   read.
7. **The honest caveat, highlighted too**: "Some of the unformalized results could have issues". She shows the
   limitation from the source itself.
8. **The verification**: a dark code repository listing **scrolling** fast (scrolling means "there are a lot"),
   with the caption "…have passed!".
9. **The other side**: a second outlet's headline with "draws concerns from experts" highlighted.
10. **Why it matters**: back to her own visual world (prime numbers with "* SOURCE: MATHVISUALPROOFS", the Sun).
11. **Outro**: face, pointing, lime "FOLLOW" pill.

The evidence order is: claim, then coverage, then primary source, then the paper itself, then the
check, then the critics, then what it means. Each document is on screen for 2-3 s. One or two phrases are
highlighted, always the words in the voice-over at that moment. Captions over white pages get a dark outline so
they still read.

**Patterns that repeat across all six reels (a series identity):**
- **Bookends**: the face opens with the claim or question, and the face closes. The outro is the same each time:
  she points at the viewer, a lime "FOLLOW" pill pops (sometimes with a small cursor clicking it), and the
  caption reads "FOLLOW ALONG!". The closing face is often a tighter punch-in than the opening one.
- **Mid-roll follow pill** at about 25-45 % of the length (9 s in a 36 s reel, 23 s in 51 s, 26 s in 56 s). It
  sits over the visual, above the caption, for about a second.
- **Little face time in the middle**: in 3 of the 5 reels the face appears only at the start and the end.
  Everything between is visuals.
- **The lime accent everywhere**: tags, the arrow, big keyword titles, the highlighter on documents, the follow
  pill. A second tag colour (blue, red) appears only when two things are compared.
- **Familiar units for every big number**: elephants, Mount Everest, the Burj Khalifa, a German shepherd.
- **Source lines** ("* SOURCE: NASA", "SOURCE: ICECUBE/NSF") on every real image, top left, tiny.
- **Each reel has its own visual world** (3D glass and grid, a flat illustration, a real sky plus 3D), and
  stays inside it, except for documents and archive, which are evidence.
- **Captions identical in all six**: small white bold capitals, one line, no box, low-middle, with a dark outline
  added only over light backgrounds.

### 2.2 The wider field
- **Hooks.** The swipe decision is made in about 1-2 s. Figures vary by source and most are vendor blogs, so treat
  them as directional ([Billo: hook-rate benchmarks](https://billo.app/creative-insider/data-insights/hook-rate-benchmarks/),
  [dev.to: hook engineering](https://dev.to/bulkdl/hook-engineering-the-science-of-the-first-3-seconds-in-short-form-video-2026-2jp9)).
  MrBeast's leaked production guide treats the first minute of long videos as where most viewers are lost, and
  over-invests there ([Alexander Jarvis copy of the memo](https://www.alexanderjarvis.com/memo-how-to-succeed-in-mrbeast-production/),
  [Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/91193170/7-key-takeaways-mrbeast-leaked-hr-document)).
  The short-form version of that is: the claim in the first sentence, and the picture of it by second 3.
- **Pacing.** Guides recommend a visual change every 1-5 s and **one idea at a time**, then resetting attention
  with a new angle or proof. They warn that a *uniform* rhythm underperforms because viewers stop noticing it
  ([Opus: Shorts length and format](https://www.opus.pro/blog/ideal-youtube-shorts-length-format-retention),
  [Subscribr](https://subscribr.ai/youtube-strategy/editing-youtube-shorts-more-engagement)). Educational shorts
  can run 35-45 s when the payoff is clear.
- **Vox / Johnny Harris explainers.** Layered compositions (a static background with moving mid- and
  foreground), highlighter marks, maps, charts, paper and film textures, grain, light leaks. Slightly choppy,
  handmade motion rather than synthetic smoothness. Small motion details give the rhythm
  ([PremiumBeat: replicating Vox](https://www.premiumbeat.com/blog/replicating-vox-motion-graphic/),
  [Motion Array: Johnny Harris tips](https://motionarray.com/learn/premiere-pro/edit-documentary-in-premiere-pro/),
  [aescripts: how Johnny Harris makes maps](https://aescripts.com/learn/how-johnny-harris-makes-maps)).
- **Caption styles.** There are two schools:
  1. *Explainer/journalist* (Cleo, Vox, Johnny Harris): small, calm, one line, no box, the same all the way through.
  2. *Punchy talking head* (Hormozi and copies): big all-caps heavy sans (Montserrat Black, Anton, "The Bold Font"),
     black outline, word-by-word pop, one yellow keyword (#F7C204) per sentence
     ([Submagic](https://www.submagic.co/blog/how-to-make-alex-hormozi-captions),
     [Blitzcut: caption fonts](https://blitzcutai.com/blog/best-caption-fonts-tiktok)).
     By 2026 this package is so common that it reads as template-made
     ([Blitzcut: caption trends 2026](https://blitzcutai.com/blog/tiktok-caption-trends-2026)).
- **Safe zones (9:16 Reels).** Keep text and faces inside about y = 210-1600 px and x ≤ 996 px of 1080×1920. The
  bottom margin varies most (the caption and buttons take 280-340 px)
  ([Remotion: social safe zones](https://www.remotion.dev/elements/overlays/social-safe-zones/),
  [Trymypost: Reels safe zones](https://www.trymypost.com/blog/instagram-reels-safe-zones-text-placement-2026)).
- **Typography trends.** Kinetic type (text moving with the voice) is the most-cited trend for 2026. Serif and
  retro type and variable-font weight shifts are smaller threads
  ([Opus: 11 editing aesthetics 2026](https://www.opus.pro/blog/editing-aesthetics-dominating-short-form-2026),
  [Pixflow 2025](https://pixflow.net/blog/top-video-editing-trends-2025/)).
- **Sound.** Whooshes land on the visual hit, to the frame. Layered impacts (sub + punch + transient) sound great
  in solo but turn to mush on a phone. **The same whoosh on every cut makes a video feel templated.** Pre-lap the
  next shot's sound before the cut. Duck music under speech. Mix in this order: voice, then music, then SFX. Aim
  for about -14 LUFS for the web
  ([SFX Engine: Reels audio tips](https://sfxengine.com/blog/reels-audio-editing-tips),
  [Sonilo: whoosh guide](https://sonilo.com/ai-music/whoosh-sound-effect-guide),
  [iZotope](https://www.izotope.com/en/learn/echoic-audio-part-2)).

Note on sources: storyboard conventions come from production-software docs and film-school guides, which agree.
Most creator "data" comes from tool vendors' blogs, so the numbers are guidance, not platform facts. The Cleo
breakdown is first-hand (frames sampled from the reel).

## 3. What Creatro does now (as read on 20 Nov 2026)

- **Playbook** ([src/autoedit-playbook.md](../../src/autoedit-playbook.md), "Storyboard first" and steps 1-5).
  It asks for a shot every 2-4 s, a hook in the first 2 s ("a bold title or a striking visual"), at least 4
  layouts, one annotation per 5 s, one SFX per 2-3 s, titles with `effect: shadow` as the default, and captions
  as "bold white text on a dark rounded pill" by default, at 1-3 words a page. There is no story structure
  (beats), no shot sizes and no rule tying a claim to a visual.
- **Shot format** ([src/storyboard.ts](../../src/storyboard.ts)). It has `kind`, `camera` (still, zoom-in,
  zoom-out, punch-in, pan-left, pan-right, shake), `transition` (cut, whip, zoom, fade, slide, glitch, flash),
  `layers` with `enter`/`to`/`note`, `sound`, `why`, `focus` and `sketch`. There is no shot size, no beat, no
  separate action line and no tilt or push-in (dolly). `EFFECTS` holds none, shadow, outline, glow, extrude,
  gold and chrome; there is no highlight or tag style. `FONTS` lists 25 Mac families, mostly display and serif;
  the heavy grotesks creators actually use (SF Pro Black and Heavy, Helvetica Neue Condensed Black) are there
  only as weights.
- **Sketch** ([app/storyboardSketch.ts](../../app/storyboardSketch.ts)). It traces footage as pencil hatching.
  All movement is in one red: the camera's end frame as a dashed box at 0.58-0.68 of the frame, four corner
  arrows, impact ticks, ghosts, speed lines, burst marks and cursor ticks. Labels ("SLOW ZOOM IN", "FADES IN",
  "TYPES ON") and layer notes are hand-lettered in Marker Felt *inside* the frame. On big views the notes are
  whole sentences.
- **Sheet and PDF** ([app/Storyboard.tsx](../../app/Storyboard.tsx), `pdfHtml`). A4 landscape with a cover page
  (title, idea, palette, look, captions, music, shot ribbon). Sheets hold 4 tall or 6 wide shots, two to a row,
  frame on the left and text on the right: number, clock and duration, the line in quotes, kind · camera ·
  transition · ♪ sound, "→" moves in red, "Shows:" and the italic why.

### 3.1 The three screenshots of shot 03 ("THE REAL DRIVER")
1. **Too much red at once.** The panel has: a zoom box that is nearly the whole frame (so it reads as a second
   border, not as "we end here"), four corner arrows, a red outline round the chip box, a dotted "FADES IN" frame,
   three red tick lines and a down arrow on the saw, a cursor tick through the title, and "SLOW ZOOM IN",
   "FADES IN" and "TYPES ON" labels. A professional panel would show the zoom and *maybe* one element move.
2. **Words across the picture.** "a round circular saw blade spins fast and drops down through" and "the two
   halves slide apart and a gold chip glows between the" are cut off, and they run across the car and the chip,
   the subject of the shot. On a professional board these sentences are action notes under the panel.
3. **Labels on the subject.** "FADES IN" sits on the chip. The zoom box's bottom edge and the type cursor cross
   "THE REAL DRIVER".
4. **Felt-pen lettering** (Marker Felt) reads as a kids' note rather than production markup.
5. **The caption pill** is the CapCut default look. Neither creator school uses it now.
6. **The user's own marks** (yellow arrow plus note box) work well and read clearly *because* everything else
   is pencil. More of the sketch should be quiet like that.

## 4. Patterns worth copying

1. **One idea per panel, words under the frame.** The frame shows the picture plus movement arrows and at most
   2-3 short capital labels. Everything else goes below as typed text.
2. **Camera at the edge and thick; element motion inside and thin.** This is the Storyboard That and StudioBinder
   convention, and it separates the two kinds of red without a second colour.
3. **The end frame as corner brackets** (crop marks) instead of a full dashed rectangle. It reads as "the frame
   we end on" and covers far less of the drawing.
4. **Numbered callouts.** A small ① in the frame next to the thing, with ① "Saw spins and drops through the car"
   in the notes under it. Comics and film boards both do this, and it removes every sentence from the picture.
5. **The AV split under each panel.** PICTURE (shot size, camera, action, on-screen text), then SOUND
   (VO "…", SFX, MUSIC), then CUT/WHIP TO between panels.
6. **Beats, not just shots.** HOOK, SETUP, TURN, PROOF, PAYOFF, CTA as bands on the sheet. Cleo's reel follows
   exactly that shape, and it helps the user judge the story, not just the frames.
7. **Visual first.** Every claim gets a concrete picture the viewer can read in 1 s (the flask labelled H₂O,
   the rings around the star).
8. **A consistent visual world and one accent colour.** The same background world across visual shots. The
   accent is used only for labels, the hero title and the CTA.
9. **Labels as tags on objects**, in the accent colour, rather than titles floating in space.
10. **Calm captions in a fixed place**, without a box, for explainers. Big punchy captions only when the user
    asks for that style.
11. **Hard cuts by default.** Transitions only at section changes.
12. **Sound on meaning, not on quota.** A different sound for different movements, pre-laps into new sections,
    and no identical whoosh on every cut.
13. **Source credits** in tiny text whenever a real photo, paper or data set is shown.

## 5. Prioritised changes

P1 = do first: biggest visible gain for the least work. Each item names the file. Items in
`app/storyboardSketch.ts`, `app/SketchPad.tsx` and `src/storyboard.ts` overlap with the session working on
editable labels and placeholder shapes, so they should be applied there or after it lands.

### P1: Make the panels read like a professional board
1. **Sentences out of the frame** (`storyboardSketch.ts` `note()`, `drawShot`). Inside the frame, draw a numbered
   callout dot (①, ②) next to each layer that has a `note`. Print the notes under the panel in the sheet card, the
   drawer and the PDF ("① Saw spins and drops through the car"). Inside the frame, allow only movement labels of
   1-3 words.
2. **One camera move plus at most two element moves drawn per panel** (`drawShot`). If more layers move, draw the
   two most important, for example the largest or the hero title, and list the rest as callouts. Per element,
   pick **one** sign: an arrow *or* speed lines *or* a ghost, never all three.
3. **End frame as corner brackets** (`camera()`): four L-shaped crop marks at the end frame, with the four
   corner arrows kept short, from the frame edge toward the brackets. Make the end frame tighter so it reads as
   a destination: slow zoom 0.75, punch-in 0.5, aimed at `focus`.
4. **Weights for the two kinds of motion** (`camera()`, `motion()`). Camera marks at full stroke, at or near the
   frame edge. Element marks about 60 % of that weight, inside, beside the element. Same red.
5. **Labels never on the subject or a title** (`label()`). Add the `focus` area and every `text` layer's box to
   `taken` before labels are placed. If no spot is free, move the label to the notes under the panel (the code
   already drops it; it should move it instead).
6. **Movement label font** (`HAND`). Replace Marker Felt with a condensed production capital: `"DIN Condensed"`
   or `"Avenir Next Condensed" 600` in red, small and tracked out (+0.06 em). Keep a hand feel through the wobbly
   *lines*, not the letters.

### P1: Defaults in the playbook (wording to paste into `src/autoedit-playbook.md`)
7. **Hook rule** (replace "Give the video a hook in the first 2 s (a bold title or a striking visual)"):
   > The hook is the claim, said in the first sentence, with the picture that proves it on screen by second 3.
   > Open on the speaker's face when the claim is personal or surprising, or on the visual when it is striking.
   > Never open with a title card, a logo, a greeting or "in this video".
8. **Visual first** (add to "Storyboard first"):
   > Decide each shot's picture before its words: for every claim, what concrete object or diagram would make
   > it understandable in one second (water = a flask labelled H₂O, a range = coloured rings)? Give the video one
   > visual world (one background, one material, one lighting) and keep every visual shot in it.
9. **Captions default** (replace "default: bold white text on a dark rounded pill…"):
   > Default (explainer): one line of 3-5 words, white, bold, all capitals, about 42 px on a 1080-wide frame,
   > no box, a soft shadow, always at the same height (about 78 % down, inside the safe zone), the same style all
   > the way through. Punchy style (only when the brief or reference asks for it): 1-3 words a page, big heavy
   > capitals, outline, the `accent` word in the accent colour.

   Step 2's "1-3 words per page" becomes "3-5 words per page (explainer) or 1-3 (punchy)".
10. **Title effects** (titles paragraph). Change "shadow (the default)" to:
    > `none` by default: flat colour on a clean background reads most premium. `shadow` only when the title sits
    > over busy footage. gold, chrome, extrude and glow are hero effects: at most one title per video, and only
    > when the mood asks for it.

    Change the same default in the sketch's `title()` and in `src/storyboard.ts` docs.
11. **Labels as tags** (annotations bullet in step 5):
    > Name things where they are: a small tag in the accent colour stuck to the object (black text on an accent
    > box, slightly tilted), or text that follows the diagram's shape. Show a source line in tiny text
    > ("SOURCE: NASA") top left whenever a real photo, paper or chart appears.
12. **Relax quotas that push towards template look** ("The bar"):
    - "At least 4 different full-frame layouts" becomes "**2-4 layouts used with intent**: the speaker for claims
      and turns, full-frame visuals for explanations; add a split or card only when two things must be seen
      together."
    - "SFX typically 1 per 2-3 s" becomes "**a sound on every meaningful visual event**, different sounds for
      different moves, never the same whoosh on every cut; pre-lap the next section's sound."
    - Add: "**Hard cuts by default**; a whip, zoom or flash only at a section change, at most 2-3 per short."
    - Add: "**One accent colour** (`look.palette[2]`) for labels, the hero title and the CTA; everything else
      neutral."

### P2: The PDF and sheet look like a production document (`app/Storyboard.tsx`, `pdfHtml` and the sheet card)
13. **AV notes under or beside each frame**, in this order with small mono headings:
    `PICTURE` (shot size · camera · action), `ON SCREEN` (titles and labels, real words), `SOUND`
    (VO "…" · SFX · MUSIC), plus ①② callout notes. The `why` moves to a single grey line at the end, or only
    to the app.
14. **Transition marks between panels**: a small "CUT", "WHIP →" or "FADE" chip in the gutter between two frames,
    instead of inside the tags line.
15. **Beat bands**: a thin labelled rule over each run of shots (HOOK 0:00-0:03, SETUP, TURN, PROOF, PAYOFF,
    CTA), on the sheet and in the PDF. The cover ribbon gets the same colours.
16. **Vertical layout option**: for 9:16, try **4 frames across with notes under each** (filmstrip reading,
    like Boords' vertical layouts) and compare it with today's 2×2 frame-beside-text layout. Keep whichever
    prints larger frames. Wide formats keep the current layout.
17. **Footer and legend**: each sheet footer shows `Title · Draft n · date · page x/y`. The cover gets a small
    legend: "red at the edge = camera, red inside = element motion, ① = note below, yellow/white/blue/green = your
    marks". Add an approval line ("Approved ____ date ____"). Shots changed since the last draft get a small
    "REV" mark in the corner.
18. **Shot shorthand in the tags line**: "CU · PUSH IN · CUT" instead of "On camera · Slow zoom in", with the
    full words on hover in the app.

### P2: Shot format (`src/storyboard.ts`, coordinate with the other session)
19. Add `size` (`ecu`, `cu`, `mcu`, `ms`, `ws`, `ews`, `ots`, `pov`, `insert`), `beat` (`hook`, `setup`, `turn`,
    `proof`, `payoff`, `cta`) and `action` (one short sentence: what happens). All optional with `.catch`, so old
    boards still parse.
20. Add camera moves `push-in` and `pull-out` (camera moves, versus zoom = lens), `tilt-up`, `tilt-down` and
    `whip-pan`. Draw them per section 1.3: the frame-within-frame for push and pull, a curved side arrow for tilts.
21. Add an `EFFECTS` entry `tag` (accent box behind the words, Cleo's labels) and `highlight` (a marker stroke
    behind a word, the Vox look). These are things creators actually use, unlike chrome and extrude.
22. `checkStoryboard` gains warnings (not errors). The first shot is longer than 3 s or has no `line`. A shot is
    over 5 s with no layer entering. More than 3 non-cut transitions. More than 2 titles with hero effects.

### P3: Drawing quality and fonts
23. **Value grouping in the pencil** (`pencil()`, `sampler()`): reduce the traced picture to 3 tones (paper,
    mid hatching, dark cross-hatching) and draw the background half as strong as the subject (around `focus`, or
    the speaker silhouette). Boards read by contrast. Today every edge has the same weight.
24. **Safe-zone ticks** on 9:16 panels: two faint marks at about 11 % from the top and 17 % from the bottom, so
    titles and captions in the sketch visibly sit inside the Reels UI area.
25. **Fonts**: no install is needed for the core look. SF Pro Heavy/Black, Helvetica Neue Condensed Black,
    Avenir Next Condensed Heavy and DIN Condensed cover the creator grotesks. In the FONTS descriptions, mark SF Pro
    Heavy and Avenir Next Condensed Heavy as "captions and labels" defaults. Montserrat, Anton or Inter would need
    installing, which needs Kartik's OK first (CLAUDE.md). Recommendation: don't install them.
    Move Zapfino, Trattatello, Chalkduster and Phosphate to the end of the list with a "rarely" note so Claude
    stops reaching for them.

### P1/P2: Show the research the way Cleo does (from the five reels, section 2.1b)
26. **Evidence order in the playbook** (P1, wording for "Storyboard first"):
    > When the video is about a study, paper, announcement or news, show the evidence as documents, in this
    > order: the coverage (headline), the primary source (paper or lab page), the key number or sentence in it,
    > the paper itself (pages), how it was checked, a caveat or critic, then what it means. Each document gets
    > 2-3 s, full frame (no card, no browser bar), pushed in until the text is big and runs off the edge, with
    > the accent-colour highlighter on exactly the words being said at that moment. Show the caveat too.
27. **A `document` shot kind** (P2, `src/storyboard.ts`): `{ "kind": "document", "source": { "url" or "file" },
    "highlight": "the exact phrase", "zoom": 1.6, "scroll": false }`. `scroll: true` is the fast scroll that
    means "a lot of results". In the sketch (`storyboardSketch.ts`), draw a page as ruled pencil lines with a
    heavy headline block and the highlighted phrase as an accent-colour band, plus the push-in frame. That reads
    instantly as "a page with this line marked".
28. **Getting the page picture, behind the tool wall** (P2, `src/toolchain.ts` and `src/autoedit-tools.ts`):
    a `$T page <url> <name> [--find "phrase"]` command that saves a screenshot of the web page or PDF to
    `assets/pages/<name>.png` and the phrase's box to timeline.json. The highlight stays a live layer, never baked
    into the picture (CLAUDE.md rule 3). Electron can capture a page itself, with no install. Fallback when the
    page can't be loaded: a typeset "quote card" of the headline and phrase in a document style. Last resort: a
    title card with the source line. The highlight and the zoom live in `timeline.json`
    (`"documents": [{ "file", "highlight": {x,y,w,h}, "at", "for", "zoom" }]`).
29. **The stacked-pages beat** (P2, a layer `enter: "stack"`): several page cut-outs land one after another,
    tilted ±4°, with shadows. It works for "hundreds of papers" or "thousands of results". In the sketch, draw it
    as 3-4 offset page outlines with a small red "STACKS" label.
30. **Comparisons with a familiar unit** (P1, playbook): "Every big or tiny number gets a familiar comparison on
    screen (elephants, a skyscraper, Mount Everest, a dog): two tags in two colours, or a measurement bracket
    with a tag." This adds a `compare` note style. No new code is needed; layers already cover it.
31. **Data charts redrawn, not screenshotted** (P1, playbook): "When a source has a chart, redraw it in the
    video's style: the real data points, one vertical marker with a label at the key event, and the trend traced
    with a thick accent stroke that draws on."
32. **Series outro and mid-roll pill** (P2): store the creator's outro (face punch-in, a follow pill in the
    accent, "FOLLOW ALONG!") and the mid-roll pill (at about 30 % of the length) as a reusable **look**, saved
    once and applied to every project. The look also holds the accent colour, the caption style and the tag style.
    This could be a `look.json` the user keeps between projects, copied into each new project folder so the
    project stays self-contained.
33. **Face bookends** (P1, playbook): "Open on the speaker with the claim or question; come back to them for
    the turn ('but…') and the outro; everything else is visuals. Their final shot is a tighter punch-in than
    the first."

### Suggested order
P1 items 7-12, 26, 30, 31 and 33 (playbook only, changes every future video) → P1 items 1-6 (after the sketch
session lands) → P2 27-29 (documents as evidence: the biggest new capability for news and science videos) →
P2 13-18 (PDF) → P2 19-22 and 32 (format, saved look) → P3.
